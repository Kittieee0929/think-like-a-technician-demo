# Two-minute panel demonstration guide

The prototype is designed to show the **proposed website intervention**, not to claim that research has been conducted.

1. **Mystery Unit** — Select **Display / video path**. Explain that learners see the *symptoms* first, not the answer.
2. **Plan Before Touching** — Choose **Display / video path** and **Loose or disconnected video cable**. Enter: “The computer powers on, but the monitor does not receive a video signal.” Add these algorithm steps: monitor power, monitor input, cable inspection with devices off, reseat cable and retest. Check both safety conditions.
3. **Test and Log** — Click all four simulated tests. Point out that each test reveals one observation and automatically appears in the diagnostic log. The simulated cable issue is confirmed only after the retest.
4. **Peer Debugging** — Check the first three issues in the fictional peer log, then select **Check my feedback**. Emphasize changing one variable at a time and documenting evidence.
5. **Reveal and Reflect** — Show the case reveal, the CT skills practiced, and the reflection box. Optionally download the plain-text practice notes.

Suggested explanation for the panel:

> “Think Like a Technician uses computer troubleshooting as the learning context, but our research outcome is computational thinking: decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction, and algorithm design. This prototype demonstrates the five proposed stages. It does not collect real student data or replace our separate, expert-reviewed pretest and posttest.”

The three proposed school sites are Marcos National High School, Bagbag–Solsona National High School, and INCAT, subject to school approval and the actual CSS schedules. All demonstration test results are fictional and predetermined.
