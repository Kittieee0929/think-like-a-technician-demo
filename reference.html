Quick Reference

Keep these short guides nearby while practicing computer troubleshooting.

Before testing: safety reminders

  1. Stop if you notice smoke, damaged cables, unusual heat, liquid damage, or exposed electricity.
  2. Follow your instructor's directions. Power down and unplug equipment when a check requires handling a connection or component.
  3. Use approved electrostatic discharge (ESD) precautions before handling internal components.
  4. Never open a power supply or work on energized internal circuits.
  5. Do not change multiple components at the same time when trying to isolate a fault.
  6. Document the symptom, the hypothesis, the test, the observation, and your conclusion.

The six-part diagnostic routine

  1. Gather — Ask what changed and note visible symptoms.
  2. Divide — Identify the affected subsystem and list plausible causes.
  3. Prioritize — Choose the safest, simplest test that distinguishes causes.
  4. Observe — Record exactly what each test shows.
  5. Decide — Confirm the likely cause; revise the hypothesis if evidence disagrees.
  6. Verify and record — Confirm normal operation and write the final report.
